The leak

You are not short of leads.
You are losing them in transit.

A hundred people click. Sixty land on a page that doesn't answer the question the ad raised. Twenty fill a form. Nobody follows up until evening. Four are still interested by then, and one of those was never going to buy in your city anyway.

Nothing in that sequence shows up in your ad account. The ad account says the ad worked. Which is why the leak survives for months.

Each of those four stages is a place your money can quietly go missing. We rebuild all four as one system, and report on every one of them each week.

The money leak

The 11 PM follow-up

A message lands at night. Reply, or risk losing it? You reply — and he was asking about a city you don't operate in. That's the fourth one this week.

The buyer who walked

A serious buyer calls and you spend two minutes hunting for his last message. He decides you're disorganised, and books a site visit with someone else.

Managing four vendors

One for the page, one for video, one for ads, and you in the middle coordinating all of them. When something breaks, no single person was watching the whole funnel.

Losing to someone faster

The deal didn't go to a better broker. It went to the one whose system replied in ninety seconds while yours waited for you to be free.

Portal leads, shared five ways

You pay 99acres or MagicBricks for the lead. So do four other brokers. By the time you call, he has been called four times already and he is annoyed.

The unit that won't sell

Ninety days on the same inventory. The price is right and the location is right, but the only people seeing it are the ones already walking past the site.

The empty site visit

You block a Sunday, arrange the keys, drive out — and he doesn't show. Nobody confirmed his budget or his timeline before the visit was booked.

The month the phone doesn't ring

Referrals dry up between seasons and there's nothing underneath them. Your pipeline depends on who happens to remember you this month.
