# YouTube / Demand Gen ad scripts — v2 (developer ICP)

Written against `docs/ICP.md` v2: an Indian developer, 40–65, who has delivered several
buildings and is sitting on unsold flats in a live RERA-registered project.

**Language: English.** Indian business register — the way you'd speak to a promoter who is
called *sir* by twenty people a day and has been in business longer than you have. Direct,
unhurried, no slang, no jargon, no matey tone. Respect without deference.

**Length: 75–90 seconds**, matching the two already shot.

---

## Compliance checklist — applies to every script

- [ ] Hook lands in the first 5 seconds and does not overpromise beyond the landing page
- [ ] No unqualified guarantees. Never "guaranteed bookings" or "guaranteed leads."
- [ ] **No numbers about the viewer's business stated as fact.** Where arithmetic appears, it is
      framed as *their* number to run, not a claim about them. This matters most in Ad 3.
- [ ] No competitor named or clearly identifiable
- [ ] End card CTA reads **"Book My Audit Call"** — exactly, no "free"
- [ ] No false urgency
- [ ] Every on-camera claim matches the landing page

> **RERA note.** These ads promote AdScade's *services*, so they do not need a RERA
> registration number. Any ad we later run for a **client's project** does — the number must
> appear on the creative and the project must be registered before a rupee is spent. This is the
> hard disqualifier in the booking form for exactly that reason.

### Ad 2 — the problem, and how to save the footage

The recorded angle is *you, personally, deciding whether to reply at 11 PM*. A promoter who has
delivered eight buildings does not do that — his sales team does. As recorded, the ad describes
a life the viewer does not live, which is the fastest way to lose him in the first five seconds.

Two ways to keep the asset rather than bin it:

**Option A — re-voice, keep the footage.** The visuals (evening, phone lighting the face) still
work; only the ownership of the pain changes. New VO below as **Ad 2R**. If the piece was shot
to camera rather than as voiceover, the lip sync won't hold — in that case use Option B.

**Option B — run it at a secondary segment.** Keep it exactly as shot and point it at
brokerages with sales teams, which is the ICP the original scripts were written for. Worth doing
only if you intend to sell to that segment too. If you don't, retire it.

## Ad 1 — "The Money Leak"  *(already shot — reference text)*

**Angle:** spend leaving every month with no visibility into which stage destroys the return.
**Status:** live asset. Text below is for reference and for any re-record.

> *[Promoter at a desk, ad account or sales report open, unimpressed]*
>
> **(Hook — 0:00)** "Every month the marketing bill gets paid. And every month, nobody can tell
> you exactly which part of it worked."
>
> **(Body)** "Here's how it usually goes. One freelancer builds the landing page. Someone else
> shoots the video. A third person runs the ads. None of them has spoken to the others.
>
> So the ad runs, the clicks come — and somewhere between that click and someone actually
> standing inside the flat, most of it disappears. The ad account shows you the click. It cannot
> show you the drop-off. Which is why the leak survives for months.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> We do it differently. Landing page, video, ads, and the WhatsApp follow-up — one system, one
> team. And every Monday you get the numbers that matter: how many enquiries came in, how many
> matched your budget and configuration, how many booked a site visit, and what each of those
> cost you."
>
> **(CTA — final 8 seconds)** "If you want to find where your funnel is leaking, book an audit
> call. The link is below."
>
> **End card:** "Book My Audit Call" · AdScade

## Ad 2R — "The Enquiry That Dies Overnight"  *(re-voice of the shot asset)*

**Angle:** the gap between when a buyer is interested and when anyone replies.
**Target length:** ~85 seconds. **Note:** the pain belongs to the *system*, never to the viewer.

> *[Evening. Phone screen lighting the face. Same footage as recorded.]*
>
> **(Hook — 0:00)** "A serious buyer enquired about your project at eleven last night. Your team
> replied at ten this morning. In those eleven hours, he visited two other websites."
>
> **(Body)** "This is nobody's fault. Your sales team was asleep, as they should have been. The
> problem is that there was nothing awake in between.
>
> And it isn't only the eleven hours. When your team does call back, they're starting cold —
> no budget, no configuration, no timeline, no idea whether this person can afford the flat or

> questions before your team ever picks up the phone. By the time a name reaches your sales
> head, the budget, the configuration and the timeline are already on record.
>
> Your team stops chasing. They start closing."
>
> **(CTA — final 8 seconds)** "If your enquiries are going cold overnight, book an audit call.
> The link is below."

## Ad 3 — "The Carrying Cost"  *(new — the strongest angle for this buyer)*

**Angle:** unsold inventory is not idle, it is expensive. This is the arithmetic script and it is
the one most likely to out-perform everything else. Delivery should be calm and factual — the
number does the work, so the voice must not.

**Target length:** ~80 seconds.

> *[Promoter walking through a finished but empty flat. Unhurried. No music, or very little.]*
>
> **(Hook — 0:00)** "This flat is finished. It's been finished for seven months. And it has been
> costing you money every single one of them."
>
> **(Body)** "Not in the obvious way. In interest.
>
> Take whatever your construction finance costs you — most developers I speak to are somewhere
> between eleven and fourteen percent. Now take one unsold flat and hold it for a year. Do that
> arithmetic on your own numbers, then multiply it by however many units are still unsold in
> your project.
>
> I'll wait.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> That number is almost always larger than the entire marketing budget you have been arguing
> about internally. And unlike the marketing budget, it runs whether anyone is selling or not.
>
> The obvious answer is to cut the price. But cutting the price marks down every remaining unit,
> annoys the buyers who paid full, and tells your market the project is in trouble.
>
> Buying demand is usually cheaper than discounting it. That's the whole argument."
>
> **(CTA — final 8 seconds)** "If you want to work out what your unsold units are actually
> costing you, book an audit call. The link is below."
>
> **End card:** "Book My Audit Call" · AdScade

> ⚠ **Compliance:** the eleven-to-fourteen percent range is stated as *what developers tell us*,
> and every calculation is explicitly handed to the viewer to run on their own figures. Do not
> change this to a claim about their business, and do not put a rupee figure on screen.

## Ad 4 — "Whose Pipeline Is It?"  *(new — channel partner dependence)*

**Angle:** he pays 2–5% and still doesn't control demand. Careful tone — channel partners are
his colleagues, not his enemy. The script must not insult the network he depends on.

**Target length:** ~80 seconds.

> *[Promoter in the office. Sales board or project display behind him.]*
>
> **(Hook — 0:00)** "Ask yourself an uncomfortable question. If every channel partner stopped
> pushing your project tomorrow, how many enquiries would you have on Monday?"
>
> **(Body)** "For most developers the honest answer is close to none.
>
> That isn't a criticism of channel partners — they do their job, and they do it well. But they
> have a portfolio, and they lead with whichever project pays best that month. When yours isn't
> the one, your pipeline simply stops. You pay two to five percent, and you still don't control
> the tap.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> What we build is demand you own. Your own landing page, your own video, your own ads, and a
> WhatsApp sequence that qualifies every enquiry before it reaches your sales team.
>
> The partners keep selling — nothing changes there. The difference is that their commission now
> buys you extra volume instead of the entire pipeline."
>
> **(CTA — final 8 seconds)** "If your project sells only when someone else decides to sell it,
> book an audit call. The link is below."
>
> **End card:** "Book My Audit Call" · AdScade

## Ad 5 — "The Last Twenty Flats"  *(new — the tail)*

**Angle:** launch demand is easy, the tail is where projects stall. Directly mirrors the "tail
that won't move" section on the landing page.

**Target length:** ~75 seconds.

> *[Promoter with a floor plan or inventory sheet, marking off what's gone.]*
>
> **(Hook — 0:00)** "The first sixty flats more or less sold themselves. It's the last twenty
> that decide whether this project made money."
>
> **(Body)** "Launch has its own energy. Channel partners are interested, your existing buyers
> are talking, and the best units go first.
>
> Then it thins out. What's left is the odd floor, the odd facing, the units nobody was leading
> with. Partners have moved on to the next launch. Your sales team is calling the same tired
> database. And every month those units stay unsold, the interest keeps running.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> The tail doesn't need a discount. It needs demand aimed at it specifically — people who want
> that configuration, at that price, in that location, in the next few months. That is a
> targeting problem and a follow-up problem, and both of those are solvable.
>
> One team builds the page, the video, the campaign and the qualification, and you see every
> Monday exactly how many site visits it produced and what each one cost."
>
> **(CTA — final 8 seconds)** "If your tail has stopped moving, book an audit call. The link is
> below."

## Running order

Test in this order — strongest hypothesis first, per `docs/ICP.md` §10:

1. **Ad 3 (Carrying Cost)** vs **Ads 1 (Money Leak)** at equal budget. This is the ICP test. If
   carrying cost wins, the developer profile is confirmed and the remaining scripts should be
   rewritten around arithmetic rather than frustration.
2. Add **Ad 4 (Channel Partner)** and **Ad 5 (The Tail)** once a winner emerges.
3. **Ad 6** and **Ad 2R** are the follow-up-speed pair — run them against each other, not
   against the inventory angles, since they argue the same point differently.

All six end on the same card, so nothing needs re-editing when you rotate creative.
