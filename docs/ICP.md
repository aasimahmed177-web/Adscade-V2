# Ideal Client Profile — AdScade

**Offer:** The Complete Buyer Funnel System — done-for-you landing page, video creative,
Meta ads, and automated WhatsApp qualification for Indian real estate businesses.

**Traffic source:** YouTube / Google Demand Gen (Hinglish video ads)
**Conversion event:** Booked free audit call
**Founder / on-camera:** Aasim Ahmed

---

## 1. The one-line ICP

> An owner-operator real estate broker in an Indian Tier-1 or strong Tier-2 market, already
> spending ₹50,000–₹3,00,000 a month across portals, Meta ads and freelancers, with live
> inventory to move, who cannot tell you their cost per *qualified* lead — and who is
> personally still answering WhatsApp at 11 PM.

Everything below is elaboration on that sentence. If a prospect fails the sentence, they
fail the ICP.

*rhythm* of the copy should read the way the ad sounds. English words, Hinglish cadence.

## 3. Psychographics — who this person actually is

**Age 28–45. Male-skewing but not exclusively. Self-made or second-generation family
business.** They did not come from marketing. They came from sales. They can close a buyer
across a table in twenty minutes and they know it — which is exactly why the marketing
failure stings. It is the one part of the business where their instinct doesn't work.

They are not lazy or cheap. They have already spent real money trying to fix this. They have
hired the freelancer. They have bought the portal package. They have run the ads themselves
after watching a YouTube tutorial at 1 AM. **Every one of those attempts failed in a way they
could not diagnose**, and that is the wound the offer presses on.

What they actually want, underneath "more leads":

- **To stop being the bottleneck.** Every lead currently routes through their personal phone.
- **To look professional to a serious buyer.** Losing a ₹2 crore buyer because they took four
  minutes to find the last WhatsApp thread is the memory that keeps them up.
- **To know where the money went.** Not more spend. *Legible* spend.
- **To hand it to one person and stop coordinating.** They are tired of being the project
  manager for three freelancers who have never spoken to each other.

What they are afraid of:

- Being sold to again by another agency with a deck and no delivery.
- Signing a retainer and getting ghosted after month one.
- Paying for "branding" they can't measure.

## 4. Qualifying signals

### Strong fit — the more of these, the better

- Already spending on ads, portals, or freelancers, **but cannot state their cost per
  qualified lead**
- Has active inventory or a project launching in the next 60 days
- Has a sales team, or personally follows up — someone is waiting for these leads
- Has been burned by at least one freelancer or agency already
- Complains about lead *quality*, not lead *volume* — this is the single highest-signal
  sentence a prospect can say
- Talks in terms of site visits and closures, not clicks

### Disqualify — politely, on the call

- **Zero ad budget.** We are not a performance-only shop. No budget, no funnel.
- **No inventory.** Nothing to sell means nothing to advertise.
- **Shopping on price alone.** "Kitna sasta kar sakte ho" is a losing client at any price.
- **Wants leads by tomorrow.** The build is one week, but the optimisation period after
  launch is two to three. Someone who cannot wait that out will not survive it either.
- **Resale-only individual agents with no listings of their own.** Too small, wrong economics.
- **Anyone asking for guaranteed closures.** We qualify leads. We do not close their deals.

## 5. Awareness and sophistication

**Awareness stage: problem-aware, solution-unaware.** They know the money is leaking. They do
not know that "one team owning the whole funnel" is a category of thing they can buy. This is
why the ad leads with the leak and not with the service — the ad's job is to name the problem
precisely enough that they feel *seen*, and the landing page's job is to introduce the
category.

**Market sophistication: stage 3–4.** They have heard "we run ads for real estate" from
fifteen people. That claim is dead. The live differentiator is **ownership of the whole
system**, and the proof of it is the reporting — CPL, CPQL, drop-off by stage.

This drives the entire page structure. The page does not argue "ads work." It argues
"scattered execution is why yours didn't."

All five ads end on the same card: **"Book My Free Audit Call"**. That exact string is the
only CTA text allowed on the page. Ad-to-page mismatch is the most common cause of Demand Gen
disapproval, and the compliance checklist in the ad script calls for exact match.

## 7. Objections, in the order they arrive

1. *"Setup fee kyun?"* — The landing page, video and automation are built for your project,
   not templated. It also means we're both committed before a rupee goes to ad spend.
2. *"Ads to main bhi chala sakta hoon."* — You can. The ad was never the problem. Running ads
   without a funnel is pouring water into a leaking bucket.
3. *"Pehle bhi agency try ki thi."* — Almost certainly one that ran ads and handed you raw
   form fills. We hand you leads whose budget, location and timeline are already on record.
4. *"Kitne leads guarantee?"* — We guarantee the system goes live in three weeks and that we
   keep optimizing free until it produces leads matching your criteria. We don't guarantee a
   number, and anyone who does is guessing.
5. *"Kitna time lagega?"* — One week to live. Days 1–2 strategy, days 3–5 build, days 6–7 test
   and launch. Then two to three weeks of optimisation while the campaigns settle.

## 8. What this means for the page

- **The visitor arrives warm but skeptical.** They watched 45–90 seconds of Hinglish video
  that named their exact pain. The page must confirm the ad was not a bait-and-switch within
  the first screen — same headline, same face, same CTA.
- **The single job of the page is one booked call.** No newsletter, no PDF, no second offer.
- **Qualification happens on the page, not on the call.** The form's job is to filter, so the
  call is with someone who already passes the ICP. A shorter form would convert higher and
  waste more of Aasim's time — that trade is deliberately made in favour of fewer, better
  calls.
- **Every claim must be one AdScade can defend.** No fabricated client counts, no invented
  percentages. Where proof does not exist yet, the page uses a credibility statement instead
  of a fake number.
