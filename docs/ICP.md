# Ideal Client Profile — AdScade

**Offer:** The Complete Buyer Funnel System — done-for-you landing page, video creative,
Meta ads, and automated WhatsApp qualification for Indian real estate developers.

**Traffic source:** YouTube / Google Demand Gen (Hinglish video ads)
**Conversion event:** Booked audit call (self-scheduled via Calendly)
**Founder / on-camera:** Aasim Ahmed

> **Status: working hypothesis, not validated.** This profile is Aasim's assumption — an Indian
> builder, 40–65, who has delivered many flats. It is coherent and probably right, but no closed
> deals have confirmed it. §10 lists what would confirm or kill it. Until then, treat the
> disqualifiers as firm and the psychographics as provisional.

---

## 1. The one-line ICP

> The owner of an Indian development firm, 40 to 65, who has delivered several buildings and is
> now sitting on unsold flats in a live project — paying interest on every one of them, watching
> possession dates approach, and dependent on channel partners who push whichever project pays
> them best this month.

Everything below elaborates that sentence. The load-bearing word is **unsold**. This buyer's
problem is not awareness and not leads — it is *inventory that costs him money every day it
stands empty*.

## 2. Who this is, concretely

| Attribute | Target |
|---|---|
| Role | Founder / MD / promoter of a development firm. Not a broker, not a channel partner. |
| Age | 40–65. Centre of gravity around 48–55. |
| Track record | Has **delivered** multiple projects — roughly 3–20 buildings, several hundred to a few thousand flats |
| Currently | 1–3 live projects, with unsold inventory in at least one |
| Team | In-house sales team of 3–20, plus a channel partner network |
| Origin | Usually civil engineering, contracting, or a family land-holding business. Rarely marketing. |
| What they sell | ₹35 lakh – ₹2.5 crore flats. Mid-market and premium residential; occasionally plots or commercial. |
| Marketing budget | ₹1,00,000 – ₹15,00,000 a month, often booked per project rather than monthly |
| Sales cycle for us | 2–6 weeks. Slower and more formal than a broker's. |

### Who actually runs the evaluation

The biggest operational difference from the broker profile. **The builder decides, but he rarely
runs the process.** Expect one of:

- A **marketing manager** who will be on the call and whose job is partly to look competent
- The **son or daughter** now in the business — 25–35, digitally fluent, often the one who saw
  the ad and forwarded it
- A **sales head** who cares about lead quality because his team's numbers depend on it
- Sometimes a **CA or CFO** scrutinising the spend

Consequence: whoever books the call may not be the person who signs. The page and the call must
both give that person something to forward upward. **A number they can defend in a review
meeting is worth more than a clever line.**

### Geography — priority order

1. **Strong Tier 2 (best fit):** Indore, Nagpur, Jaipur, Lucknow, Bhubaneswar, Coimbatore,
   Surat, Vadodara, Nashik, Raipur, Chandigarh/Mohali, Kochi, Visakhapatnam
2. **Tier 1 peripheries where mid-market volume lives:** Thane / Panvel / Dombivli, Greater
   Noida, Gurugram sectors 80–113, North Bengaluru, Hyderabad ORR belt, Pune PCMC & Hinjewadi
3. **Tier 1 core:** possible, but these firms usually have in-house marketing or a retained
   agency, so the sale is against an incumbent

Tier 2 matters more here than it did for brokers. These firms have real budgets and real
inventory but no in-house marketing function — exactly the gap we fill.

### Language

Hinglish in ads and on calls, but a **more formal register** than for a young broker. This is a
man called *sir* by twenty people a day, who has been in business longer than the person selling
to him has been working. Respect without deference. No slang, no matey tone.

The page stays in English — it is a vendor-evaluation document that may be forwarded to a
marketing manager or a CA — but the ads should sound like a peer, not a vendor.

## 3. Their economics — why the pain is real

Get this right and the funnel writes itself. A builder's problem is arithmetic, not mood.

**Unsold inventory has a carrying cost.** Construction finance from a bank or NBFC runs roughly
11–14%. A ₹60 lakh flat unsold for twelve months is on the order of **₹7 lakh of interest** —
gone, on one flat. Multiply by forty unsold units and the number dwarfs any marketing budget he
will ever argue with us about. **This is the most persuasive number available to this offer, and
it is his own number, not ours.**

**RERA changed the clock.** Registered projects carry committed possession dates; 70% of
collections sit in escrow and can only fund construction; slipping the date brings penalties
plus interest to buyers. Sales velocity is therefore not a revenue question — it directly funds
the build. Slow sales → slow construction → delayed possession → penalties → reputation damage,
in a market where reputation is the only durable asset.

**Discounting is the trap he wants to avoid.** Cutting price to move ageing stock devalues every
remaining unit, angers buyers who paid full price, and marks the project as distressed. He would
far rather pay for demand than cut price — which is exactly what we sell. Say so plainly.

**The last 25% is the hardest.** Launch absorbs the easy demand from channel partners and
existing networks. The tail — odd floors, odd facings, the units nobody led with — is where
projects stall and where marketing actually earns its fee.

**Channel partner dependence is structural.** He pays 2–5% brokerage and still does not control
demand. Partners push whatever project pays best this month. He has no owned pipeline and no
idea what a booking really costs him.

> Note the direction of the relationship: he **sells through** brokers and channel partners. He
> is not one. Getting this backwards in copy loses him in a single sentence.

## 4. Psychographics

**He is not a marketer and does not pretend to be.** He is an operator — land, approvals,
contractors, RERA filings, bank relationships, delivery. He respects people who know their
domain because he knows his. He will not be impressed by jargon and will be actively suspicious
of anyone using it to sound clever.

**He has been pitched to death.** Portal enterprise packages, digital agencies, PropTech
platforms, "performance marketing" outfits, a nephew who did a course. He has probably paid for
at least one and received a spreadsheet of enquiries his sales team could not convert. **His
scepticism is earned, and it is specifically about lead quality.**

**He measures in site visits and bookings.** Not clicks, not impressions, not even leads. A lead
is a phone number; a site visit is a person standing in the flat. If our reporting talks in CPL
and CTR we sound like the last three vendors. If it talks in **cost per site visit** and **cost
per booking**, we sound like someone who understands his business.

What he wants, underneath "more customers":

- **Demand he owns**, so channel partners become a channel rather than a dependency
- **To clear ageing inventory without cutting the price**
- **Site visits from people who can afford the flat** and are buying inside his timeline
- **A number he can take to a review meeting** and defend
- **To not look desperate.** Brand matters more to him than to a broker; he has a name in the
  city and the next project depends on it

What he fears:

- Paying for enquiries again and having his sales head report they were junk
- Marketing that makes the project look like it is not selling
- A vendor who disappears after the launch campaign
- Being another logo on an agency's deck

## 5. Qualifying signals

### Strong fit

- **Unsold inventory in a RERA-registered live project** — the precondition for everything
- Has delivered projects before: a track record to market and no approval risk
- Dependent on channel partners, and says so with some frustration
- Cannot state cost per site visit or cost per booking
- An in-house sales team waiting on qualified footfall
- Ageing inventory in a project past its launch phase — the tail units
- Already on portals, receiving shared, low-intent enquiries
- A next project in the pipeline, so this becomes a relationship rather than one job

### Disqualify

- **Project not RERA-registered.** Advertising an unregistered project is illegal under RERA
  §3. Not a preference — we cannot run these ads at all. **Hard stop. Ask first, always.**
- **No unsold inventory.** Nothing to sell, nothing to build a funnel for.
- **Land aggregators or first-time developers with nothing delivered.** No track record to
  market, high approval risk, usually no budget until the first sale.
- **Wants us on pure commission per booking.** We are not a brokerage. Our fee is for building
  and running the system.
- **Expects channel partners replaced in month one.** The funnel supplements the network; it
  does not substitute for it overnight, and promising that would be a lie.
- **Distressed and wants a discount campaign.** If the plan is to cut price and shout about it,
  they need a liquidator, not a funnel.
- **Wants footfall by next weekend.** Build is one week; the campaign needs two to three more
  to find the audience.

## 6. Awareness and sophistication

**Awareness: solution-aware, vendor-sceptical.** Unlike the broker, he knows digital marketing
for real estate exists — he has bought it. What he does not believe is that anyone will do it
*accountably*. The ad's job is therefore not to introduce the category; it is to sound like the
first vendor in five years who talks about the right metric.

**Market sophistication: stage 4, edging to 5.** Every claim in the category is burnt out. "We
generate leads" is noise. "We generate qualified leads" is nearly noise. The live differentiator
is **one team owning the whole funnel, reported in site visits and bookings** — and the proof is
the report, not the promise.

This is why the weekly report is the product's centre of gravity rather than the last item on a
deliverables list. Show it early.

## 7. Message-to-market match — and a problem

| Ad script | Emotional driver | Fit for a builder |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — The Money Leak | Wasted spend, no visibility | **Good.** Works nearly as written. |
| 2 — The 11 PM Follow-Up | Personal exhaustion answering leads | **Wrong person.** A builder does not chase leads at 11 PM; his sales team does. |
| 3 — The Buyer Who Almost Walked | Looking disorganised to a buyer | **Reframe.** It is not him fumbling for a message — it is his sales team, and it is his brand. |
| 4 — Death by a Thousand Freelancers | Vendor coordination fatigue | **Partial.** He does juggle vendors, but has staff to absorb it. Weaker driver. |
| 5 — The Deal You Lost to Someone Faster | Losing to a faster competitor | **Good, reframed** as losing a booking to a competing project down the road. |

> ⚠ **The biggest open item in the project.** The five scripts were written for brokers and
> agents. On this ICP, **script 2 is aimed at the wrong person entirely** and scripts 3 and 4
> need reframing. Two of five is not a rounding error — it is 40% of the ad spend pointed at a
> pain this buyer does not have.
>
> The strongest angles for a builder are **not in the current set at all**: the carrying cost of
> unsold flats, the tail of the project that will not move, discounting as the thing to avoid,
> and channel partner dependence.
>
> Recommendation: keep 1, reframe 3 and 5, retire 2, and write two new scripts on carrying cost
> and channel partner dependence. **This needs a decision before spend goes live.**

## 8. Objections, in the order they arrive

1. *"Portal pe already listed hai, aur agency bhi try ki thi."* — Both give you enquiries. This
   gives you site visits, and tells you what each one cost. A different unit of measurement.
2. *"Channel partners already bech rahe hain."* — And they will keep selling. This is demand you
   own, so their commission buys incremental volume instead of the whole pipeline.
3. *"Setup fee kyun?"* — The landing page, video and automation are built for your project, not
   templated. It also means both sides are committed before a rupee goes to media.
4. *"Kitni bookings guarantee?"* — None, and anyone quoting a number before seeing your
   inventory, price and market is guessing. What we commit to is the system live in a week and
   optimisation at no extra charge until it produces leads matching your criteria.
5. *"Mere paas already marketing manager hai."* — Good — he is who we report to. We are the
   build and the media, not a replacement for him.
6. *"Price kam karna padega to?"* — That is the outcome this exists to avoid. Buying demand is
   cheaper than a 5% cut across every unsold unit. Run the arithmetic together on the call.

## 9. What this means for the page

- **The visitor arrives sceptical, not warm.** He has been sold to before. The page must prove
  domain fluency in the first screen — say *unsold inventory*, *site visit*, *possession*,
  *channel partner*, and he knows we have done this before.
- **Lead with arithmetic, not emotion.** For the broker profile the hook was exhaustion. For a
  builder it is carrying cost. Emotion still works, but it must sit on top of a number.
- **Report in his units.** Cost per site visit and cost per booking are the headline metrics;
  CPL and CPQL are supporting detail, not the promise.
- **Ask about RERA registration in the qualification form.** It is a legal precondition, and it
  is also a fast, credible signal that we know the business.
- **Write for a forwarded page.** A marketing manager or a son may be reading it and passing it
  up. Give them a defensible number, not only a persuasive feeling.
- **The only CTA string is "Book My Audit Call"**, and it is the only CTA text permitted
  anywhere on the page.

> ⚠ **Open item.** All five ad end cards currently read "Book My Free Audit Call". "Free" was
> removed from the page on client instruction, so **the end cards must be re-cut** before spend
> goes live.

## 10. How to validate this profile

It is an assumption. Three cheap tests, in order:

1. **Ten calls.** Ask every prospect: how many delivered projects, how many unsold units, is the
   project RERA-registered, what does a site visit cost you today, and who else is on this call.
   If most cannot answer the unsold-units question instantly, the carrying-cost thesis is wrong.
2. **Who books.** Track whether the form is filled by the promoter or by a manager or family
   member. If it is mostly managers, the page and ads should speak to a manager's incentives —
   looking competent, having numbers for the review — which is a different pitch.
3. **Which angle converts.** Run script 1 (money leak) against a new carrying-cost script at
   equal budget. If carrying cost wins, this profile is confirmed and the rest of the script set
   should be rewritten around it.

Until test 1 is done, keep targeting broad and treat every disqualifier in §5 as firm. The
disqualifiers are the part of this document least likely to be wrong.
